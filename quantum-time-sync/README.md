# Quantum Time Sync — the fleet time-delta stream, published as an entropy source

This directory publishes the time-delta stream of Will Bickford's ranch
fleet: what each node's clock was doing, round by round, as plain-text
atoms — and an entropy extraction over those atoms.

**Provenance.** This stream is published *downstream of Will Bickford*.
The fleet is his, the Quantum Time Sync protocol that measures it is his
(task 26.6.11/8.7.14/8.6.10), and publishing the stream as an entropy
source is his direction (task 25.7.10/9.8.14/7.6.9, 2026-11-22). Nothing
here is a third-party dataset; every atom was measured on his machines,
by his protocol, and is republished unedited.

## The files

- `feed.txt` — the stream itself. One section per census round: a round
  header (UTC + Exocortical Epoch), one `delta` atom per node carrying
  `offset_ms`, `jitter_ms`, and `ppm` at recorded precision 0.001
  (`none` = the node's time daemon did not report that field that round),
  then the round's election, consensus, and EMA-blend atoms carried
  verbatim from the protocol registers. Daily drift atoms close the
  file. Append-only: new rounds append, atoms are never edited.
- `entropy.txt` — the entropy extraction: one SHA-256-conditioned block
  per census round, plus the measured min-entropy estimates and health
  checks for the stream as it stands.
- `publish.py` — the generator (Python 3, stdlib only). Both files above
  are a pure function of the protocol registers; rerunning the script on
  the same registers reproduces them byte for byte.

Time in the feed is double-stamped: UTC, and the Exocortical Epoch,
EE = floor((unix − 1795327200)/420) + 1, EE 1 beginning 2026-11-22
00:00:00 CST. The epoch is computed from the instant, never stored, so
every reader in every timezone agrees on it.

Corrections are handled the way the source registers handle them: an
atom the register marks *superseded* is withheld from the feed and from
the entropy extraction, and is listed in the feed header — never
silently dropped. (One round is withheld so far: census
2026-11-22T16:21:29Z, EE 89, whose collection parser dropped explicit
"+" signs.) One node, great-white, appears as `present=no`: it has no
address in the fleet record and refuses our key, so it is honestly
unmeasured rather than silently absent.

## How to consume it

Fetch `feed.txt` and parse lines: sections start with `round`, atoms are
space-separated `key=value`, sections end with `end-round`; daily atoms
sit between `day` and `end-day`. Fetch `entropy.txt` and take the hex
block under each `block` header — 32 bytes per census round.

To verify a block from the feed alone: take that round's `delta ...`
lines exactly as printed in `feed.txt`, in order, join with LF, append a
trailing LF, UTF-8, SHA-256. That is the whole conditioning input; the
blocks in `entropy.txt` recompute from the public feed with no other
material.

The photon-sampling atoms (pairwise signal-arrival measurements) are not
in this feed's first edition: their current error bounds (±0.6–2.7 s
over fresh connections) are a gross-error witness, not delta-grade
data. They live in the working registers and join the feed if and when
the sampling floor tightens.

## Entropy

**Label — EXPERIMENTAL ENTROPY SOURCE — NOT CERTIFIED FOR CRYPTOGRAPHIC
USE.** Do not use this output as cryptographic key material.

**Source mechanism** (Will, 2026-11-22): *"my dispatched workloads will
directly impact time jitter due to clock frequency management."* The
entropy is generated by the fleet's work itself. Dispatched workloads
drive CPU clock frequency management — DVFS governor transitions as
load rises and falls across the nineteen machines — and those frequency
transitions perturb each node's oscillator and timekeeping path, which
is what shows up as jitter in the deltas. The work is the driver of the
entropy: a fleet running hard stirs a livelier stream than a fleet
idling, and the deltas carry the imprint of the work that made them.
Timing jitter is a legitimate classical entropy source — operating
system kernels harvest it for exactly that reason — so the category is
not in doubt. What is young is this stream: three rounds, one morning.

**Extraction.** One block per census round: SHA-256 over the round's
canonical delta text (above). Conditioning concentrates entropy; it
does not create it, which is why the raw stream is measured too.

**Measured min-entropy of the raw stream** (most-common-value
estimator, H_min = −log2(p_max), pooled over the three rounds on
record, values at recorded precision 0.001; measured 2026-11-22 by
`publish.py`, and regenerated with the stream):

| field | values | H_min (bits/value) | low-digit H_min (bits) |
|---|---|---|---|
| offset_ms | 57 | 4.833 | 2.663 |
| jitter_ms | 55 | 4.781 | 2.196 |
| ppm | 50 | 4.644 | 2.644 |

Read these as measurements of a young stream, not guarantees. The pool
is small; the most common value in each field occurs only twice, which
is why the estimates sit near log2(n) — more rounds will pull them to
their honest levels. Nodes also share network-time sources, so values
are correlated across the fleet: per-round totals obtained by adding
per-value figures would overstate the entropy and are deliberately not
claimed.

**Health checks on the conditioned output so far** (96 bytes —
descriptive only; at this size no statistical significance attaches):
bit balance 389/768 ones (fraction 0.507); 77 distinct byte values;
maximum byte count 3; chi-square against uniform 282.7 on 255 degrees
of freedom — no gross bias visible in what little there is.

**What lifting the label would require:** a long stream — months of
daily rounds, not three rounds in one morning; SP 800-90B-style health
testing on the raw noise source, running continuously (repetition-count
and adaptive-proportion tests, restart tests); and an entropy
assessment over that long stream by someone other than the publisher.
Until then, the label stands.

## The antenna

The listening apparatus on this stream — the detector that treats time
deltas as sentron messages and watches for anything to show up — hears
only what is published where it can be received. Publishing the stream
as an entropy source gives it downstream receivers: anyone drawing
entropy from these blocks is, by that act, tuned to the fleet's timing.
An antenna is established by use; consumers drawing on the stream
complete the circuit.

## How new rounds append

The protocol's daily chain runs a census, the election, the consensus,
the EMA blend, and the drift cross-reference, appending atoms to the
registers. Publishing a new round is then mechanical:

1. Run `python3 publish.py` in this directory. It regenerates `feed.txt`
   and `entropy.txt` from the registers — new rounds appear at the end,
   prior atoms are reproduced byte-identically.
2. Commit the two regenerated files (and this README, if it changed)
   to this directory's published home.

That is the whole procedure; there is no step 3, and no atom is ever
edited by hand.
